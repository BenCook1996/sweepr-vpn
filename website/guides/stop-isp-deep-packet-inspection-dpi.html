1. How Residential DPI & Token-Bucket QoS Throttling Classify Traffic

Even when you browse HTTPS websites, the initial TLS handshake exposes the destination domain name in clear text inside the Server Name Indication (SNI) extension of the ClientHello packet. Inline Deep Packet Inspection (DPI) appliances positioned at broadband network gateways (such as residential UK networks on AS5089 and AS2856) parse those SNI strings alongside QUIC Initial headers and sustained UDP flow heuristics.

Once an automated DPI classifier tags a flow as 4K video CDN traffic, peer-to-peer transfer, or cloud backup during 19:00–23:00 peak hours, upstream QoS policers apply a token-bucket rate limiter. Bursts pass briefly while the bucket drains, after which packets are queued or tail-dropped—triggering buffering stalls and TCP congestion backoff even on gigabit lines.

2. Why ChaCha20-Poly1305 WireGuard (udp/51820 & udp/443 Stealth) Neutralizes SNI Profiling

WireGuard replaces verbose protocol headers and plaintext TLS certificates with a silent Noise_IKpsk2 handshake and minimal 4-byte packet type framing. Every inner IP header, TCP/UDP port, DNS lookup, and TLS SNI field is authenticated and encrypted using ChaCha20-Poly1305 before leaving your device:

  • Standard WireGuard (udp/51820): Zero plaintext SNI strings, zero X.509 certificate metadata, and line-rate kernel encryption on 10Gbps bare-metal nodes running fq_codel Active Queue Management.
  • Stealth Mode (udp/443): Moves outer WireGuard encapsulation onto UDP port 443 (identical to HTTP/3 QUIC transport), clearing restrictive campus, enterprise, or mobile carrier firewalls that block port 51820 while keeping packet overhead minimal.

Frequently Asked Questions

If my browser already uses HTTPS, why can my ISP still throttle specific video or gaming sites?

HTTPS encrypts page payloads after the handshake, but standard TLS 1.2 and unencrypted TLS 1.3 ClientHello packets transmit the hostname inside the plaintext SNI field, allowing ISP DPI gear to trigger per-domain QoS shapers.

When should I choose udp/443 Stealth over default udp/51820?

Use udp/51820 at home for maximum native throughput; switch to udp/443 Stealth whenever hotel, university eduroam, or cellular carrier firewalls drop UDP 51820 handshakes or selectively police VPN ports.
