1. Roku OS: Disabling Smart TV Experience & Catching Hardcoded DNS

On Roku TVs and streaming sticks, navigate to Settings → Privacy → Smart TV Experience and uncheck Use info from TV inputs, then open Settings → Privacy → Advertising and check Limit ad tracking (plus click Reset advertising identifier).

Crucially, Roku OS firmware attempts direct queries to Google's 8.8.8.8 and 8.8.4.4 even when DHCP hands out a custom DNS IP. Without a router firewall DNAT intercept rule redirecting port 53 to 10.64.0.1, calls to cooper.logs.roku.com, scribe.logs.roku.com, and amoeba.roku.com slip straight past Pi-hole or ISP DNS filters:

# OpenWrt / Linux router: Force hardcoded Roku DNS (8.8.8.8) into SWEEPR sinkhole 10.64.0.1
iptables -t nat -A PREROUTING -i br-lan -p udp --dport 53 -j DNAT --to-destination 10.64.0.1:53
iptables -t nat -A PREROUTING -i br-lan -p tcp --dport 53 -j DNAT --to-destination 10.64.0.1:53

2. Vizio SmartCast: Stopping Inscape Frame & Audio Fingerprinting

Vizio embeds its subsidiary Inscape directly into the SmartCast display pipeline. Even if you ignore SmartCast apps completely and watch TV purely via an Apple TV or game console over HDMI-1, Inscape samples video frames and audio waveforms every second and posts telemetry to inscape.tv, tvinteractive.tv, cognitivetv.com, and analytics.vizio.com.

Disable it via All Settings → Admin & Privacy → Viewing Data → Off (or System → Reset & Admin → Smart Interactivity → Off on older Vizio models), choose Reset Advertiser ID, and sinkhole all four Inscape domains at the router layer so SmartCast updates cannot quietly re-enable ACR.

3. Amazon Fire OS: Blocking device-metrics-us.amazon.com Safely

On Fire TV Stick 4K Max and Fire TV Cube devices, go to Settings → Preferences → Privacy Settings and turn off Device Usage Data, Collect App Usage Data, and Interest-based Ads. Under Settings → Preferences → Featured Content, disable Allow Video Autoplay and Allow Audio Autoplay to stop background carousel preloads.

At the network boundary, sinkholing device-metrics-us.amazon.com, device-metrics-us-2.amazon.com, fls-na.amazon.com, fls-eu.amazon.com, and aax-eu.amazon-adsystem.com halts Fire OS usage uploads while explicitly safelisting primevideo.com, aiv-cdn.net, and pv-cdn.net so Prime Video streams in full 4K UHD. Roku, Vizio & Fire TV Privacy FAQs

Why does Roku still reach cooper.logs.roku.com after I change DHCP DNS on my router?

Roku players hardcode 8.8.8.8 as a fallback/primary resolver inside Roku OS. Unless your router uses a port-53 NAT redirect (or routes the entire Roku subnet inside a WireGuard tunnel), telemetry queries bypass standard DHCP DNS assignments.

Does Vizio Inscape collect viewing data even when I use an external HDMI streamer?

Yes. Because Inscape sits at the television panel's input scaler rather than inside an individual app, it hashes video frames from cable boxes, Blu-ray players, consoles, and external sticks unless Viewing Data is disabled and inscape.tv is sinkholed.

Will sinkholing device-metrics-us.amazon.com break Prime Video playback or Alexa search?

No. SWEEPR's safelist keeps primevideo.com, aiv-cdn.net, and pv-cdn.net completely unblocked while dropping only Fire OS metrics and ad-system telemetry collectors.
