1. How Samba Interactive TV Operates on Sony Bravia Google TV

Sony Bravia XR and Bravia OLED televisions built on Android TV and Google TV frequently ship with Samba TV's Automatic Content Recognition (ACR) SDK pre-installed as a privileged system service. During initial setup—or immediately following a major firmware upgrade—the TV prompts users to enable Samba Interactive TV for show recommendations.

Once permitted, the background service continuously samples audio and visual fingerprints directly from the hardware framebuffer, covering cable boxes, PlayStation HDMI ports, and broadcast tuners alike. Those frame hashes are correlated with household IP addresses and dispatched to sambametrics.com, tracking.samba.tv, api.cid.samba.tv, agn.fe.ca.sambatv.com, and legacy flingo.tv collectors, while Bravia Core and Google TV track app telemetry separately through advertising identifiers.

2. Turning Off Samba TV & Bravia Core Ad IDs in On-Screen Menus

  1. Disable Samba Interactive TV: Open the Sony Bravia home screen and navigate to Settings → Privacy → Samba Interactive TV (on older Android TV builds: Settings → System Preferences → Samba Interactive TV). Switch the master toggle Off.
  2. Purge Your Google TV Advertising ID: Navigate to Settings → Privacy → Ads and choose Delete advertising ID, then disable Usage & Diagnostics under the same Privacy section so crash telemetry stops reporting app names.
  3. Opt Out of Sony Bravia Core & Sony Select Personalization: Open Settings → About → Legal Information → Privacy Policy and revoke optional marketing analytics, and disable BRAVIA Notifications / Sony Select telemetry inside app permissions.

3. Disabling Bloat Telemetry Packages via ADB & Blocking Samba Domains on Your Router

Power users who want the Samba daemon completely frozen in userspace can enable Developer Options on Bravia Google TV (press Build seven times under Settings → System → About), turn on USB / Network Debugging, and inspect telemetry packages over Android Debug Bridge (ADB) from any workstation on the same subnet:

# Connect to your Sony Bravia TV IP over local LAN
adb connect 192.168.1.45:5555

# List Samba TV, Sony Select, and Bravia analytics packages
adb shell pm list packages | grep -Ei "samba|bravia|sony|metrics"

# Disable Samba Interactive TV for user 0 without rooting
adb shell pm disable-user --user 0 tv.samba.interactive
adb shell pm disable-user --user 0 com.sony.dtv.braviasyncservice

# Router / WireGuard DNS sinkhole rules (10.66.0.1 or Pi-hole)
0.0.0.0 sambametrics.com
0.0.0.0 tracking.samba.tv
0.0.0.0 api.cid.samba.tv
0.0.0.0 preferences.cid.samba.tv
0.0.0.0 agn.fe.ca.sambatv.com
0.0.0.0 flingo.tv
0.0.0.0 sonyselect.tv

Pair ADB package hardening with router-layer WireGuard DNS sinkholing (10.66.0.1) so system updates or factory-reinstated packages remain permanently isolated from external Samba brokers.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will disabling tv.samba.interactive via ADB survive a Sony Bravia Google TV OS upgrade?

ADB pm disable-user --user 0 flags usually persist across minor security patches, though major Android TV version jumps or new regional packages can introduce updated Samba package names. Combining ADB with router DNS sinkholing at 10.66.0.1 ensures zero packets escape regardless of package updates.

Does blocking sambametrics.com and tracking.samba.tv break Google Cast or Bravia Core movies?

No. Google Cast discovery operates locally over multicast mDNS (224.0.0.251:5353), while streaming media CDNs remain completely separate from Samba's tracking subdomains.
